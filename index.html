Read it top to bottom. Nevada’s PCFP provides $3.56B: $3.22B enters the General Fund and $338M goes to separate weighted funds. The General Fund’s $3.22B includes $383,965,616 labeled “PCFP – Local Special Education.” It is already part of the General Fund resources, not another payment into the Special Education Fund. Budget pp. 2, 5 ↗

What the new branch shows. The $726.6M Special Education Fund receives $572.7M from the General Fund and $153.9M directly from the state’s Special Education Services Account. Its spending is $619.9M in special programs, $104.4M in support services and $2.3M in interdistrict payments. Budget pp. 16–22, 104 ↗

Special education also appears outside this fund. The General Fund includes a separate $28.4M special-programs line shown above. Federal grants, including IDEA, support special education through other accounts; the $2.4M General Fund federal node is not CCSD’s total federal funding. The $726.6M is a fund total, not a districtwide total for all special education services. Budget pp. 4, 79–89 ↗ · CCSD special education finance ↗

Scope and rounding. This map uses the FY2026 Final Budget adopted May 19, 2025, for the year ending June 30, 2026; these are planned amounts, not actual spending or a later amended budget. The General Fund’s 56% / 44% split refers to roughly $3.206B in spending, excluding transfers and the ending balance. About $0.427M in General Fund facilities spending is included in that total but not separately drawn. Carryover & Other includes $663.2M opening balance and $50.45M other financing sources. The $193M ending balance is retained, not spent. Food service, capital projects and other funds are not fully expanded here. CCSD budgets $6.85B in expenditures across governmental funds. Figures are rounded; totals use the budget’s reported subtotals.
