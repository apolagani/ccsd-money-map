How an IEP turns into dollars
  1. Evaluation & IEPA team evaluates the student. If they qualify, the team writes an IEP listing the instruction, related services and supports they get.
  2. Counted for state aidEach student with an IEP counts toward state special ed aid, up to 13% of enrollment. Students past that count at half rate.
  3. Money reaches the fundThat count drives the $162.0M in state aid. The General Fund transfers $737.2M to cover the rest so the fund balances.
  4. Services deliveredThe fund pays for IEP instruction, related services, transportation, supervision, and supplies and equipment.

Not every special ed dollar follows the IEP count. The $453M “PCFP Special Education” amount inside the General Fund was set from past local spending; it is $453.03M in FY2026 actuals and in both the FY2027 final and amended final budgets. Federal IDEA grants are mostly allocated from late-1990s child counts plus population and poverty, not this year’s IEP count. Read it top to bottom. Nevada’s PCFP provides $3.70B: $3.39B enters the General Fund and $310M goes to separate weighted funds. The General Fund’s $3.39B includes $453,025,801 of “PCFP – Local Special Education.” It is already part of the General Fund resources, not another payment into the Special Education Fund. A further $162M from the state’s Special Education Services Account goes straight to the Special Education Fund. Schedules B-1 and BB, pp. 2, 5

What the special education branch shows. The $899.2M Special Education Fund receives $737.2M from the General Fund and $162.0M directly from the state’s Special Education Services Account. Its spending is $789.3M in special programs and $109.9M in support services. No interdistrict payments are budgeted. Schedules BB, BB-2 and T, pp. 16–22, 106

Where IEPs fit. An IEP is the legal plan that spells out a student’s special education services. In 2024–25, 42,899 CCSD students had one, 14.49% of 296,145 students on validation day. State law funds students with disabilities up to 13% of enrollment, with half funding above that, so roughly 4,400 CCSD students fall past the cap. The per-student figures divide FY2027 budget amounts by that 2024–25 count, so treat them as rough averages, not funding rates. Nevada Report Card 2024–25 ↗ · NRS 387.122 ↗

Special education also appears outside this fund. The General Fund includes a separate $8.2M special-programs line shown above. Federal grants, including IDEA, support special education through other accounts; the $3.2M General Fund federal node is not CCSD’s total federal funding. The $899.2M is a fund total, not a districtwide total for all special education services. CCSD special education finance ↗

Scope and rounding. Every budget figure on this map comes from the “Amended Final Approved” column of the district’s FY2027 Amended Final Budget state forms (forms revised November 19, 2026), for the year ending June 30, 2027; these are planned amounts, not actual spending. The General Fund’s 56% / 44% split refers to $2.984B in spending, excluding transfers and the ending balance. Other support is $0.48M of facilities spending. Carryover & Other is the $423.6M opening balance, which equals the audited balance at June 30, 2026, plus $37.6M of other financing sources. The $179M ending balance is retained, not spent. The amended budget is larger than the Final Budget approved in May 2026: General Fund resources rose from $3.886B to $3.913B, and the Special Education Fund from $786.5M to $899.2M. Food service, capital projects and other funds are not drawn; CCSD budgets $8.93B net across all funds. The budget assumes total enrollment of 270,004. IEP counts come from the 2024–25 Nevada Report Card, not the budget. Figures are rounded; totals use the budget’s reported subtotals. CCSD Open Book ↗ · CCSD financial reports ↗

IEP math: 13% × 296,145 = 38,499 students fully funded; 42,899 − 38,499 = 4,400 over the cap. $899,215,044 ÷ 42,899 = $20,961 per student with an IEP; $161,994,636 ÷ 42,899 = $3,776 of direct state aid per student. These mix an FY2027 budget with a 2024–25 count, and the state uses its own enrollment counts for funding, so they are approximations. Instruction is 99% salaries and benefits ($485,989,032 + $255,056,947 of $748,621,039).

General Fund reconciliation: $2,984,041,548 in expenditures + $750,301,166 in transfers + $178,602,497 in ending balance = $3,912,945,210 in resources (a $1 source rounding difference).
